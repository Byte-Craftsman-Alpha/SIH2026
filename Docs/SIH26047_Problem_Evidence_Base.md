## 1. THE 2-MINUTE CONSULTATION

**Data point:** India's average primary-care physician consultation length is **~2 minutes** — among the shortest in the world. The systematic review covered **111 publications, 179 studies, and 28,570,712 consultations across 67 countries**; global range: 48 seconds (Bangladesh) to 22.5 minutes (Sweden). **18 countries representing ~50% of the global population** have average consultations of **5 minutes or less**. Shorter consultations are significantly associated with more ambulatory-care-sensitive hospital admissions, lower physician satisfaction, and higher workload/stress.

- **Source:** Hobbs FR et al., *"International variations in primary care physician consultation time: a systematic review of 67 countries,"* **BMJ Open** 2017;7:e017902 (peer-reviewed, S)
- **Published:** 8 November 2017
- URLs: https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/7/10/e017902 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29118053/
- Corroborating coverage: The Times of India, 9 November 2017 — "Doctors in India see patients for barely 2 minutes: Study" (A): https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/doctors-in-india-see-patients-for-barely-2-minutes-study/articleshow/61570077.cms

## 4. HISTORY-TAKING DRIVES THE MAJORITY OF DIAGNOSES

**Data point:** Across five landmark studies spanning 1947–2009, the medical history alone led to the final diagnosis in **56%–88%** of cases; pooled mean **75.9% (95% CI 67.9–83.9)**:

| Study | History-alone diagnostic yield |
|---|---|
| Platt, The Lancet (1947) | 74% (68–74/100) |
| Hampton et al., BMJ (1975) | 82.5% (66/80) |
| Peterson et al. (1992, replicated Hampton) | 76.3% (61/80) |
| Journal of the Association of Physicians of India (2000) | 78.6% (77/98) |
| Wang et al., UCLA neuro-ophthalmology clinic (2009) | 88% (of 115) |

Modern corroboration: a 2024 JMIR study found medical history contributes **~80%** to a diagnosis, and a 2025 *Nature* paper (Google AMIE) notes "**60–80% of diagnoses are made through clinical history-taking alone**."

- **Source (pooled review):** Zander D., *"A History of Patient History-Taking"* — peer-reviewed, NYU Grossman School of Medicine, Clinical Correlations
- **Published:** 26 January 2022 · https://www.clinicalcorrelations.org/2022/01/26/a-history-of-patient-history-taking-a-brief-review-of-the-origins-of-the-aphorism-that-80-of-diagnoses-can-be-made-by-history-alone/
- **Source (JMIR 2024):** *Importance of Patient History in AI-Assisted Medical Diagnosis*, JMIR Medical Education 2024 — https://mededu.jmir.org/2024/1/e52674/
- **Source (Nature 2025):** Tu T. et al., *"Towards conversational diagnostic artificial intelligence,"* Nature (2025) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08866-7

> **Pitch line:** "We are not automating the least valuable part of the visit — we are securing the *most* valuable part: the 75–80% of diagnostic information that today never gets captured."

## 5. DIAGNOSTIC ERROR IS COMMON — AND HISTORY-TAKING FAILURES ARE A PROVEN CAUSE

**Data point A (WHO):** Diagnostic errors occur in **5–20% of physician–patient encounters**, and "**most people will suffer a diagnostic error in their lifetime**."

- **Source:** WHO Fact Sheet — *Patient Safety*
- **Published/updated:** 13 September 2023 · https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/patient-safety

**Data point B (BMJ Quality & Safety, 2024):** Harmful diagnostic errors affect **~1 in every 14 (≈7%) general-medicine hospital patients**; **85% were preventable**. Critically for this PS: errors were significantly associated with **history-taking failures (2.5× the risk)** and uncertainty in initial assessment (4× the risk).

- **Source:** Rodziewicz TL / BMJ Group summary of the BMJ Quality & Safety single-centre study
- **Published:** 2 October 2024 · https://bmjgroup.com/harmful-diagnostic-errors-may-occur-in-1-in-every-14-general-medical-hospital-patients/

**Data point C (US multi-hospital):** In 29 hospitals, **23% of patients** who were transferred to ICU or died had experienced a diagnostic error; **17% of all patients suffered harm** from it.

- **Source:** Schnipper/JAMA Internal Medicine study, reported by Harvard Gazette
- **Published:** 9 January 2024 · https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/01/research-assesses-rates-causes-of-diagnostic-errors/

## 6. MEDICATION ERRORS & ADVERSE DRUG EVENTS IN INDIA (the allergy/history failure)

**Data point A:** A 2025 systematic literature review of **40 Indian studies** found a **median medication-error incidence of 34.11%** and error frequency of **26.74%** in Indian hospitals; errors concentrate at the **prescribing stage (40%)** — the exact stage where missing allergy/drug-history information does its damage. India is estimated to see **~5.2 million injuries annually** from medical errors.

- **Source:** Govil D. et al., *"Epidemiology of Medication Errors in Indian Hospital Settings"* — systematic review (peer-reviewed, PMC)
- **Published:** 2025 (covering Jan 2014–Apr 2025) · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12683552/

**Data point B (supporting, LMIC tertiary-care study):** **36.2% of adverse drug events were medication errors; 88% occurred at the prescribing stage; 62% of all ADEs were preventable** — the dominant patient-side risk factor being **polypharmacy without a consolidated medication list** (≥7 medications → 3.5× odds of an ADE).

- **Source:** Sahilu T. et al., *"Adverse Drug Events and Contributing Factors Among Hospitalized Patients"* — peer-reviewed (PMC)
- **Published:** 2020 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7689274/

> **Pitch line:** "A consolidated, always-present allergy and medication list is one of the cheapest patient-safety interventions that exists — and it is exactly what a 2-minute consult cannot produce."

## 7. PAPER RECORDS & RECORD LOSS (the fragmentation problem)

**Data point:** In a peer-reviewed study of urban residents in northern India (n=419): **413 of 419 respondents (98.6%) maintained paper-based records**; **96 of 351 (27.4%) had lost medical records**; **57 (16.2%) had forgotten to bring records** to a healthcare visit. Separately, EMR adoption in India is estimated at only **~35% of hospitals**, concentrated in large urban private facilities; government hospitals, small clinics and rural centres remain predominantly paper-based (vs ~96% hospital EMR adoption in the US).

- **Source:** *Perception towards electronic health records & uptake of digital health IDs among urban residents in northern India* — Indian Journal of Medical Research (peer-reviewed)
- **Published:** 2024 · https://ijmr.org.in/perception-towards-electronic-health-records-uptake-of-digital-health-ids-among-the-urban-residents-in-northern-india-a-mixed-methods-study/
- Supporting: EMR adoption comparison — https://logicloom.in/electronic-medical-records-in-india-current-state-challenges-and-future-prospects/ (October 2024)

## 8. ABDM — THE RAILS EXIST, THE FIRST MILE DOESN'T

**Data point A:** **ABHA accounts crossed 90 crore** in 2026 — growth: 14.7 Cr (2021) → 30.4 Cr (2022) → 50.6 Cr (2023) → 72.2 Cr (2024) → 84.5 Cr (2025) → **90+ Cr (May 2026)**; women are ~half of all holders.

- **Source:** PIB (Press Information Bureau), *"Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission Crosses Landmark Milestone of 90 Crore ABHA Accounts"*
- **Published:** 30 May 2026 · https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2266979

**Data point B (the asymmetry that defines our opportunity):** As of **5 August 2025**: 79.91 Cr ABHAs, 4.18 lakh facilities on HFR, 6.79 lakh professionals on HPR — but only **1.59 lakh health facilities actually use ABDM-enabled software** (as of Feb 2025), i.e., **well under half of registered facilities generate digital encounters**, and the vast majority of these records enter the ecosystem *already digitized by the provider* — not captured from the patient's speech and paper at the point of care.

- **Source:** PIB, Rajya Sabha reply, *"Update on ABDM"* — 12 August 2025 · https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2155449
- Source: PIB, *"Update on the implementation of Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)"* — 11 February 2025 · https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2101737

**Data point C (the scale of the government OPD universe):** **1,75,560 Ayushman Arogya Mandirs operational** with **371.97 crore cumulative footfall** (till 31 Dec 2024); **31.86 crore eSanjeevani teleconsultations** delivered by Dec 2024; **36.36 crore Ayushman Cards** created under PM-JAY with 8.59 crore hospital admissions.

- **Source:** Department of Health & Family Welfare, MoHFW, *Annual Report 2024–25* (Government of India)
- **Published:** September 2025 · https://www.mohfw-dohfw.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/09/45b06af4508a53a059c74efc930d955e.pdf

## 9. THE EMERGENCY "BLANK PATIENT" (justifying break-glass access)

**Data point:** In **2024 alone, India recorded 68,584 hit-and-run road accidents killing 34,030 victims and injuring 51,517** — every one of them, when survivable, is a patient whose identity, allergies, medications and comorbidities are unknown to the treating team. Total road deaths in 2024: **~1.77 lakh** (1,51,950 male + 25,225 female), with thousands of victims of unknown age/identity.

- **Source:** Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (MoRTH), Government of India — *"Road Accidents in India 2024"* (official annual report)
- **Published:** 2025 · https://morth.gov.in/backend/documents/uploaded/1781177676_V1gUW8tJWT.pdf

> **Pitch line:** "Every day, ~94 Indians survive a hit-and-run and reach a hospital as a stranger. Today that team guesses. With MediKiosk break-glass, it retrieves a medically critical summary in minutes — under full audit."

## 10. PHYSICIAN WORKFORCE DISTRIBUTION (why intake must be patient-facing, not doctor-facing)

**Data point:** India had **10,22,859 registered allopathic doctors as on 31 March 2017**, giving an allopathic doctor-to-population ratio of just **0.77 per 1,000** (per Lok Sabha answer reported in a peer-reviewed analysis) — and doctors are heavily concentrated in urban areas, which is why a single AIIMS OPD absorbs 10,000 patients/day while rural areas face scarcity. (Note: counting AYUSH practitioners, the combined ratio crosses 1:1,000 — which is precisely why this PS expects one platform to serve *both* systems.)

- **Source:** Kumar R., *"India achieves WHO recommended doctor population ratio"* — Indian Journal of Health Sciences & Biomedical Research (peer-reviewed, PMC), citing the MoS Health Lok Sabha statement
- **Published:** 2018 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6259525/

## HOW TO USE THIS EVIDENCE IN THE PITCH

1. **Open with the sponsor:** AIIA alone did 3.28 lakh OPD consultations in one year (Ayush report, Sep 2025) — "this problem lives in your own OPDs."
2. **Quantify the waste:** 2-minute consults (BMJ Open 2017) × 8,000–10,000 daily OPD load (AIIMS/Health Ministry) = the history that drives 76–80% of diagnoses (Hampton 1975 / JAPI 2000) is the first casualty.
3. **Quantify the danger:** 5–20% of encounters carry diagnostic error (WHO 2023); history-taking failure multiplies that risk 2.5× (BMJ Q&S 2024); 34% median medication-error incidence in Indian hospitals (2025 SLR) with errors concentrated at prescribing.
4. **Quantify the readiness:** 90 crore ABHAs (PIB, May 2026) but <1.6 lakh facilities on ABDM software (PIB, Feb 2025) — identity is solved, capture is not.
5. **Quantify the emergency need:** 68,584 hit-and-run accidents in 2024 (MoRTH) = thousands of "blank patients" per year for whom break-glass access is the difference between guessing and knowing.
